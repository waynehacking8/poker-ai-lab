# LLM-based and hybrid poker agents, 2024 to November 2026, and what transfers to a small non-LLM bot

Scope note: all pages below were fetched or queried on 2026-11-21 unless marked otherwise. Unit conversion used throughout: 1 bb/100 = 10 mbb/hand. "Unverified" means I saw only a search snippet or a secondary page, not the primary source. GitHub numbers come from the REST API on 2026-11-21 (`license: none` means the API returned no license object, which can still hide a license header in the files).

## Q1. RL-trained reasoning models and poker fine-tuning (SPIRAL, PokerBench, SpinGPT, ToolPoker, GRPO-on-poker): how strong are they?

### Takeaway
Every LLM-trained poker agent in this window either plays toy games (Kuhn, Leduc, Limit) or, in NLHE, reaches at best roughly break-even against Slumbot with wide error bars (SpinGPT's SFT model: +13.4 ± 12.9 bb/100 over 30,000 hands, with a hand-written stack-depth patch). The strength consistently comes from solver labels, not from the RL itself. In the one paper that tested it (ECHO-2), GRPO against a single opponent overfit to that opponent and turned wins against simple rule bots into losses.

### Cited Findings
* SPIRAL (Liu et al., arXiv 2506.24119, v1 30 Jun 2025, v3 2 Mar 2026, ICLR 2026 poster) trains Qwen3-4B/8B-Base, Octothinker-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-7B by self-play on TicTacToe, 3-card Kuhn Poker and Simple Negotiation, with 5-card Kuhn as an out-of-distribution variant. ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.24119v3); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011289))
* SPIRAL method: Role-conditioned Advantage Estimation (RAE) keeps a separate exponential-moving-average baseline per game and per role and subtracts it from the return, because the two seats have asymmetric payoffs and the opponent is non-stationary. Without RAE the paper reports "thinking collapse": traces shrink from about 2,000 characters to near zero within about 100 iterations, and math accuracy drops from 35% to 12% around step 150. ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.24119v3))
* SPIRAL compute: 8 H100, 400 steps, 128 samples per step, lr 1e-6, about 25 h per Qwen3-4B run. ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.24119v3))
* SPIRAL game strength is only measured against other LLMs: Kuhn win rate vs Gemini-2.0-Flash rises from 21.5% (base) to 48.5% (Kuhn specialist) and 53.9% (multi-game model); per checkpoint 6.25% at step 0 to 68.8% at steps 256 to 384. Training against fixed LLM opponents (Mistral, Gemini) left general reasoning below the base model (29.6 and 33.4 vs 34.0), while self-play reached 43.4. ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.24119v3)). The page has internal inconsistencies (4 vs 5 models, 53.9% vs 62.5% for the same comparison), noted by the fetch.
* SPIRAL repo: spiral-rl/spiral, 211 stars, MIT, last push 2026-03-27. ([GitHub API](https://github.com/spiral-rl/spiral))
* MARSHAL (arXiv 2510.15414, 2025) extends self-play RL on strategic games including Kuhn Poker and reports up to 28.7% improvement on held-out games for Qwen3-4B. Unverified: search snippet only. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15414))
* PokerBench (Zhuang et al., arXiv 2501.08328, 14 Jan 2025, AAAI 2025): 11,000 test spots (1,000 preflop, 10,000 postflop). Labels: preflop from GTO Wizard, postflop solved with WASM-Postflop. Released training set 60,000 preflop and 500,000 postflop spots; the fine-tuning subset used 30,000 preflop and 80,000 postflop. ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.08328v2))
* PokerBench accuracy (exact match / action accuracy, %): GPT-4 few-shot 53.55 / 65.54; Llama-3-70B few-shot 39.16 / 49.78; Llama-3-8B fine-tuned 78.26 / 80.64; Llama-2-7B fine-tuned 78.11 / 79.91; Gemma-2B fine-tuned 51.84 / 62.74. ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.08328v2))
* PokerBench gameplay: higher-accuracy checkpoints beat lower ones over 50,000 hands (step 5000 beat step 800 by 50.88 bb/100), yet the best fine-tuned model lost to GPT-4 by 22.20 bb/100 over 1,000 hands. The authors blame GPT-4's donk bets, which the solver-derived dataset under-represents, and call this a limitation of plain SFT. ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.08328v2))
* PokerBench repo: pokerllm/pokerbench, 83 stars, no license detected, last push 2025-01-15. ([GitHub API](https://github.com/pokerllm/pokerbench))
* SpinGPT (Maugin and Cazenave, arXiv 2509.22387, v1 26 Sep 2025, v2 23 Feb 2026): Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, SFT on 320,000 decisions from 8,800 high-stakes Spin & Go games (one author's own play, 2018 to 2020), then offline ORPO on 270,000 InstaGTO (CFR solver) hands plus 50,000 human hands, with the solver's argmax-EV action as the positive label. ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22387v2))
* SpinGPT results: 78% tolerant agreement with the solver. The SFT model (not the final RL model) beat Slumbot by 13.4 ± 12.9 bb/100 (95% CI) over 30,000 heads-up hands, but only after a hand-written patch because it over-shoved at 200 BB (no 200 BB training data): an all-in is replaced by a 2/3-pot bet or a 3x raise. The final model beat the SFT model by 13.2 ± 7.24 bb/100 over 10,000 duplicate hands at 25 BB. About 1.4% of SFT outputs were illegal moves. SFT took 10 h on one A100 40 GB; weights are not released. ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22387v2))
* ToolPoker (Lin et al., arXiv 2602.00528, 31 Jan 2026, ICLR 2026): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct trained by SFT then PPO to call a solver/equity API, CFR+ ground truth, Kuhn/Leduc/Limit only, no NLHE. Limit Hold'em: ToolPoker averages +45.0 chips per 100 games but loses -5.0 vs DeepCFR; Leduc -3.0 vs CFR+. Numbers carried from the sibling note `HUNL bot algorithms frontier 2026/opponent_modeling_exploitation.md`, not re-fetched in this session. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00528))
* ECHO-2 (arXiv 2602.02192, Feb 2026), Appendix F.6, No-Limit Hold'em (env id `poker://nlhe_6max_v1`, table size not stated in text): GRPO on Qwen3-0.6B moved final chip change vs the LLM training opponent from -1.677 to +1.245, but vs three unnamed rule-based bots from +0.571/+0.514/+0.592 to -0.195/-0.599/-0.451. Authors: "a trade-off that may be addressed by multi-opponent training or more diverse opponent sampling". No variance reported. ([arXiv HTML, offset 100000](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02192))
* ISO, "Implicit Strategic Optimization" (Xia et al., arXiv 2602.08041, 8 Feb 2026): a Strategic Reward Model that estimates long-run value of actions plus a context-conditioned GRPO variant (iso-grpo), tested on 6-player NLHE and Pokemon. The abstract gives no numbers; results are only "consistent improvements" over unnamed LLM and RL baselines. ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08041))

### Inferences
* No LLM RL paper in this window reports a win against Slumbot or GTO Wizard AI by an RL-trained policy. The only positive Slumbot number (SpinGPT) comes from imitation of human pro play plus solver labels, has a CI that almost touches zero, and needed a rule patch for stack depths absent from training.
* The ECHO-2 result is the most direct warning for a Chipzen bot: optimizing against one opponent made a policy lose to simple rule bots it previously beat. Train against a population that includes rule bots like the ones on the arena.
* PokerBench shows that solver-label accuracy does not guarantee results against off-distribution play (donk bets). An amateur rule-bot field is all off-distribution play, so any distilled policy needs explicit coverage of odd bet sizes and lines.

### Gaps
* No primary numbers for MARSHAL; ISO poker numbers not found in the abstract and the full paper was not read.
* ToolPoker not re-read this session; code exists only as an anonymized review link per the sibling note.
* ECHO-2 does not describe its rule-based opponents, so it is unknown how weak they were.

## Q2. GTO Wizard Benchmark (March 2026) results for frontier LLMs, and later updates

### Takeaway
Zero-shot frontier LLMs all lose to GTO Wizard AI, the best by -16 bb/100, and most are within a few bb/100 of the Always Fold baseline (-64.6). The best LLM system reported since (PokerSkill, May 2026) reaches -5.7 bb/100 with a hand-built rule library, and that rule library alone, with no LLM, scores -13.2 bb/100, which is better than every zero-shot LLM and better than Slumbot's -19.4.

### Cited Findings
* GTO Wizard Benchmark (Provost, Ilenic, Solinas, Beardsell; arXiv 2603.23660, 24 Mar 2026): public HUNL API against proprietary GTO Wizard AI, 50/100 blinds, 200 BB stacks reset every hand, AIVAT scoring (about 3x lower SD, about 10x fewer hands). GTO Wizard AI beat Slumbot by 19.4 ± 4.1 bb/100 over 150,000 hands. Strong human pros are cited at about 5 bb/100. ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660))
* LLM setup: 5,000 hands per model, zero-shot, no tools or equity calculator, JSON output with a 1 to 2 sentence reasoning field. Total cost $5,207.72 and 1,929.98 hours. ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660))
* Results, bb/100 (Table 2): GPT-5.3 XHigh -16 ± 3; GPT-5.4 XHigh -17.8 ± 3.7; GPT-5.3 High -18.2 ± 3.9; Claude Opus 4.6 -20.4 ± 8.6; Claude Opus 4.5 -22.3 ± 10.1; Gemini 3 Pro -30.1 ± 7.5; Gemini 3.1 Pro -30.8 ± 4.5; Gemini 2.5 Pro -39.2 ± 9.6; Kimi K2.5 -41.4 ± 12.5; Grok 4 High -60 ± 15.1; GPT-4o -64.9 ± 15; GPT-5.4 Mini -107.9; GPT-4 -136.2; GPT-5.4 Nano -189.7. GPT-5.4 without reasoning appears only in Table 3 at -57.50 AIVAT. ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660))
* Baselines, bb/100: Always Fold -64.6 ± 3.3; Check Call -241.1 ± 26.2; Uniform Random -284.9 ± 30; All-in -380.6 ± 4.3. ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660))
* Stated LLM failure modes: GPT-5.3 XHigh misreads about 2% of its own holdings; preflop frequencies are unbalanced (vs an SB open it 3-bets only the strongest hands and calls nearly everything else); little mixing; actions correlate with hand strength; models reason per hand about EV and do not model ranges. The benchmark measures only EV loss against a non-adapting opponent, not exploitability. ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660))
* The paper's tables have inconsistencies: GPT-4o is -64.9 in Table 2 but -60.90 in Table 3, and the Table 2 ± does not match the Table 3 AIVAT SD. ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660))
* Later public leaderboard (PokerNews, published 13 Apr 2026, updated 26 Jun 2026, snapshot "correct as of April 10, 2026") adds an entry "Marvel" from MIT at -14 ± 4.7 bb/100 over 5,090 hands, which is above GPT-5.3 but listed second; the article does not say what Marvel is. API submissions need at least 2,500 HU hands at 200 BB and are capped at 100,000 hands per month; heads-up PLO benchmarks are "planned". ([PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2026/04/gto-wizard-ai-outperforms-gpt-5-and-grok-4-in-new-benchmark-51020.htm)). The Marvel row also appears in a World Poker Deals blog dated 17 Apr 2026 (search snippet only, unverified). ([World Poker Deals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/gto-wizard-ai-crushed-llm-in-poker-heads-up))
* Benchmark client repo: gtowizard-ai/researcher-api-client, 27 stars, no license detected, last push 2026-04-09. ([GitHub API](https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/researcher-api-client))
* PokerSkill (Li, Wang, Huang; arXiv 2605.30094, 28 May 2026): a deterministic "context engine" (board texture, hand class, action-line scenario, position, SPR, size-weighted betting pressure) plus a layered rule library (frequency-annotated preflop tables for 12 scenarios at 200 BB, about 60 action-line scenarios, 23 hand classes, river blocker rules) and per-hand attack/defense "budgets" that shrink with each prior bet's size relative to the pot. The library was written by the human poker experts among the authors from GTO study; no solver runs at play time, and the paper does not say an LLM helped write it. ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30094v1))
* PokerSkill results vs GTO Wizard AI, mbb/hand (bb/100 = value / 10): GPT-5.5 XHigh with PokerSkill -57 ± 21; Opus 4.6 -80 ± 29; Opus 4.7 -87 ± 64; rule library alone, no LLM, always taking the first viable action from the budget system, -132 ± 19; GPT-5.5 XHigh default prompt -132 ± 25; GPT-5.3 XHigh default -160 ± 30; Slumbot -194 ± 41 (indirect, raw chips, from GTO Wizard's published match). At least 5,000 hands per LLM condition; the rule-only sample size is not stated, and the ± is defined as 1 SE in one place and as 95% CI in another. ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30094v1))
* PokerSkill repo: lbn187/PokerSkill, 52 stars, API license NOASSERTION but the README badge says CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial), created 2026-05-27, last push 2026-11-07. The README repeats the table (-57 ± 21 with GPT-5.5, -132 ± 19 rule-only) and says main results used 5,000 hands, "10,000 for the ablation"; it is not explicit that the rule-only row is one of those ablation runs. It also ships a Slumbot driver that reports raw winnings. ([GitHub API and README](https://github.com/lbn187/PokerSkill))

### Inferences
* Against a near-equilibrium opponent, folding everything costs about 65 bb/100 at 200 BB and any calling-station style costs about 240. Most LLMs sit close to the fold baseline, so their play is mostly "lose small", not "win". The same benchmark therefore says nothing about how they do against weak opponents.
* The PokerSkill rule-only number is the single most transferable result in this whole literature for a pure-Python bot: hand-class plus board-texture plus pressure-budget rules, with a naive action picker, reach about -13 bb/100 against GTO Wizard AI at 200 BB. That is a strong prior that a well-built rule core is a safe floor, before any learning. Caveats: authors' own result, unclear sample size, 200 BB only, and Chipzen's stacks get shallower as blinds rise.
* "Marvel (MIT)" is the only non-frontier-LLM entry on the public table and it beats every LLM. Its method is unknown.

### Gaps
* No leaderboard snapshot after 10 Apr 2026 found; whether PokerSkill or any RL-trained bot was submitted to the live leaderboard is unknown.
* Marvel's method and affiliation are not documented in any primary source I found.
* The benchmark has no short-stack or rising-blind format, so it does not test push/fold play.

## Q3. LLM agents that model opponents (Suspicion-Agent and successors): evidence of adaptation

### Takeaway
LLM opponent modeling shows clear behavioral adaptation (deviation from a baseline style, exploitation of fixed strategies) but almost no measured profit in NLHE. The one quantified exploit is CSRO in Leduc, where an LLM-written code policy whose raise EV uses an estimated opponent fold rate won 110 vs AlwaysCall against CFR+'s 62, while staying close to unexploitable vs CFR+.

### Cited Findings
* Suspicion-Agent (Guo et al., arXiv 2309.17277, COLM 2024): GPT-4 with first-order theory-of-mind planning infers the opponent's likely hand and tendencies in Leduc Hold'em; it beat neural baselines such as NFSP but fell short of CFR+, per the later ToolPoker summary. ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17277v3); [ToolPoker HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00528v1)). Snippet-level for the comparison; I did not re-read either paper's tables this session.
* Readable Minds (Lin and Hou, arXiv 2604.04157, 5 Apr 2026): 2x2 design (persistent memory x poker domain knowledge), 5 replications, about 6,000 agent-hand observations in Texas Hold'em. Theory-of-mind-like modeling reached levels 3 to 5 only with memory and stayed at level 0 without it; opponent-grounded deception appeared only with memory (Fisher p < 0.001). Memory-equipped agents deviated more from tight-aggressive play (67% vs 79% TAG adherence, p = 0.008). No win-rate or profit numbers in the abstract. ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04157))
* CSRO, Code-Space Response Oracles (Hennes, Li, Schultz, Lanctot; arXiv 2603.10098, 10 Mar 2026, AAMAS extended abstract): Gemini 2.5 Pro writes best-response policies as Python code inside PSRO. Repeated Leduc, 100 hands per game, population CFR+, AlwaysCall, AlwaysFold, 3 seeds. Returns vs CFR+ / AlwaysCall / AlwaysFold: CSRO-AlphaEvolve -4.4 / 110.3 / 42.0; CSRO-ZeroShot -19.6 / 99.7 / 41.0; PSRO with IMPALA RL oracles -58.4 / 57.7 / 40.7; CFR+ 0.0 / 62.1 / 57.4. ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10098v1))
* CSRO's best Leduc policy computes raise EV as P(fold) x pot plus P(call) x showdown value, with equity and an estimated opponent fold-to-bet frequency as inputs; it stops bluffing vs AlwaysCall and bluffs constantly vs AlwaysFold. The visible RRPS policy imports only `collections`, `random`, `typing`, so the evolved policies run with no LLM at play time (implied, not explicitly guaranteed). ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10098v1))
* GENSTRAT (Shadarevian et al., arXiv 2605.23238, v1 22 May 2026, v2 29 Oct 2026): procedurally generated zero-sum imperfect-information betting games, 12 models, more than 40,000 LLM-vs-LLM matches; ablations link larger LLM margins to more thinking budget and to disclosing the opponent's strategy. No poker numbers in the abstract. ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23238))
* Game Arena poker reveals both players' hole cards after every hand and keeps the full text history within each 100-hand episode, and its system prompt says to default to GTO and deviate only to exploit. ([arXiv HTML 2609.31473v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31473v1))
* GTO Wizard Benchmark notes its opponent does not adapt, so it cannot measure frequency mistakes or exploitation either way. ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660))

### Inferences
* The transferable piece is not "ask an LLM to model the opponent" but the CSRO structure: an explicit opponent statistic (fold-to-bet, call-down rate) feeding an EV formula for bet vs check. That is a few lines of Python and numpy and needs no model at runtime.
* CSRO also shows the cost of exploitation against a fold bot: CFR+ earned 57.4 vs AlwaysFold and the evolved policy only 42.0, so an exploit-tuned rule can leave money on the table against other types. A Chipzen bot should switch exploits per opponent type, not commit to one.
* Readable Minds suggests that cross-hand memory is the precondition for any opponent modeling. In a short Chipzen match, that means priors from offline data plus fast per-match updates.

### Gaps
* No 2025 to 2026 paper measures LLM opponent modeling profit in HUNL against rule bots.
* The CSRO Leduc policy listing was not in the visible text; only the paper's prose description was read.

## Q4. LLMs that design or evolve poker strategies or algorithms offline (program search, AlphaEvolve-style)

### Takeaway
Two DeepMind lines are real and measured, but only on small games: AlphaEvolve evolved CFR variants (VAD-CFR, distilled to WOP-CFR) with the lowest exploitability on 14 to 15 of 18 small games, and CSRO evolved runnable Leduc policies. The only NLHE-scale "LLM writes a Python poker bot" benchmark (Husky Hold'em) has no public numbers I could reach, and CodeClash reports that top LLMs lose every round to expert human programmers across its code arenas.

### Cited Findings
* "Discovering Multiagent Learning Algorithms with Large Language Models" (Li, Schultz, Hennes, Lanctot; arXiv 2602.16928, v1 18 Feb 2026, v3 7 May 2026): AlphaEvolve with Gemini 2.5 Pro mutates CFR and PSRO source code; fitness is negative exploitability after a fixed number of iterations on 3-player Kuhn, 2-player Leduc, 4-card Goofspiel and 5-sided Liar's Dice. ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16928); [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16928v3))
* Results across an 18-game suite (4 train, 14 test): VAD-CFR is top three on every game and lowest exploitability on 15; the distilled WOP-CFR is top three everywhere and lowest on 14. Baselines: CFR, CFR+, LCFR, DCFR, PCFR+, DPCFR+, HS-PCFR+(30). Exact exploitability is limited to games under about 350K information states; no hold'em, no Deep CFR comparison, single AlphaEvolve run. ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16928v3))
* Ablation (train / test log-improvement lost when removed): hard warm start, no policy averaging for the first 500 iterations, -5.256 / -2.003; asymmetric discounting of negative regrets -4.192 / -1.065; optimistic prediction -7.029 / -0.905; volatility tracking -0.842 / -0.029; regret boosting -0.954 / -0.204. WOP-CFR keeps only the first three. ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16928v3))
* CSRO (see Q3) on Leduc: AlphaEvolve-refined code reaches AggScore 44.9 ± 4.1 vs CFR+ 39.8 ± 0.3 and PSRO-IMPALA -45.0 ± 10.1; zero-shot LLM code 20.7 ± 3.0. ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10098v1))
* Husky Hold'em Bench (Kumar, Nguyen, Jin, Carlow, Quesnelle; NeurIPS 2025 workshop "Evaluating the Evolving LLM Lifecycle"): LLMs write Python `player.py` bots, refine them over a 5-stage loop with performance feedback, and play 6-player NLHE round robins of 1,000 hands, scored by net winnings. Claude 4 Sonnet "tops the leaderboard"; top models play balanced or aggressive, lower ones defensive. No margins or baseline bots on the abstract page. ([NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/122372); [repo README](https://github.com/NousResearch/huskyholdem-bench))
* Husky repo: NousResearch/huskyholdem-bench, 19 stars, no license detected, last push 2025-09-05. ([GitHub API](https://github.com/NousResearch/huskyholdem-bench))
* CodeClash (arXiv 2511.00839, v1 2 Nov 2025, v2 12 May 2026): 6 code arenas, 1,680 tournaments, 8 LMs; "top models lose every round against expert human programmers", and model repos get "progressively messy and redundant". The abstract does not name the arenas, so whether poker is one of them is unconfirmed. ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00839))

### Inferences
* For our 48-hour offline budget, the cheap transferable item is the WOP-CFR recipe (warm start without averaging, asymmetric negative-regret discount, optimistic prediction) applied to whatever abstraction we solve. Evidence is only on small games, so treat it as an A/B option against DCFR or PCFR+, not a sure gain.
* An LLM writing the bot is the setting closest to Chipzen, but there is no published evidence that LLM-written poker code beats a strong human-written bot. CodeClash's general result points the other way.

### Gaps
* Husky Hold'em leaderboard numbers: the OpenReview PDF returned a bot-check page and huskybench.com failed DNS resolution on 2026-11-21.
* No AlphaEvolve-style work on NLHE heuristics or abstractions found.

## Q5. Bot-vs-bot arenas for LLM-built or agent-built poker bots, 2025 to 2026: what won and how

### Takeaway
In LLM-vs-LLM arenas the winners are the loosest, most aggressive models (GPT-5.2 at +46.6 bb/100 with 96% VPIP in Kaggle Game Arena's 900,000 hands; GPT-5 at +143 bb/100 with 89% VPIP in a hobby arena), because the field over-folds or plays passively. The same models lose to GTO Wizard AI. No arena in this window shows an LLM-built bot beating solver-grade bots.

### Cited Findings
* Kaggle Game Arena poker was announced on 2 Feb 2026 with an 8-model heads-up NLHE bracket (finals 4 Feb 2026) and pros (Nick Schulman, Doug Polk, Liv Boeree) only as commentators. ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/kaggle-game-arena-updates/))
* Game Arena technical report (Doerschuk-Tiberi et al., arXiv 2609.31473, 25 Sep 2026): 1-2 blinds, 100 BB reset each hand, 100-hand episodes, duplicate dealing with seats and cards swapped, 20,000 hands per pairing, 45 pairings, 900,000 hands. No non-LLM baselines. ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31473v1))
* Game Arena leaderboard, bb/100 (95% CI): GPT-5.2 46.56 (40, 54); o3 29.69 (22, 38); Grok 4 27.11 (15, 39); Claude Opus 4.5 17.62 (12, 24); Claude Sonnet 4.5 12.83 (6, 20); Gemini 3 Flash Preview 5.49 (0, 12); DeepSeek V3.2 -10.05; Gemini 3 Pro Preview -15.17; Grok 4.1 Fast Reasoning -19.19; GPT-5 mini -94.87. ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31473v1))
* Game Arena style: button open frequency 52.78% (Gemini 3 Pro) to 98.27% (GPT-5 mini), six of ten above 85%; VPIP 55.16% to 96.42% (GPT-5.2); GPT-5.2 folded only 7.59% of big blinds vs a button steal; Gemini 3 Pro folded 43.78%. Authors: GPT-5 mini's "indiscriminate opening range without corresponding postflop competence is severely punished", and Gemini 3 Pro's passive defense "concedes significant equity preflop". ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31473v1))
* PokerBattle.ai (Max Pavlov; PokerNews 1 Nov 2025, updated 14 Nov 2025): 9 LLMs, play money, $10/$20 NLHE, $100,000 bankroll each, 3,799 hands over five days, models could take notes. Final: o3 +$36,691, Claude Sonnet 4.5 +$33,641, Grok 4 +$28,796, DeepSeek R1 +$18,416, Gemini 2.5 Pro +$14,655, Magistral +$3,281, Kimi K2 -$14,370, GLM 4.6 -$21,510, Llama 4 busted after 3,501 hands with VPIP above 60%. The organizer warned against reading much into the sample. The article's own figures do not fully reconcile. ([PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2025/11/poker-bot-battle-results-49971.htm))
* LLM Poker Arena (hobby project, report dated Jan 2026): 6 LLMs, 1,000-hand heads-up matches, 15 pairings. GPT-5 +143.33 bb/100 (12 rebuys, VPIP 89%, PFR 35%); Sonnet +89.66; Gemini +87.09; Grok +40.36; Mistral -42.48; DeepSeek -317.96 with 164 rebuys. No non-LLM baselines. ([LLM Poker Arena](https://mattohan567.github.io/AI-Poker-Arena/))
* dev.fun Arena: a public 6-max NLHE arena mixing frontier LLMs, OSS solver bots and equity heuristics. ### Inferences
* In fields of loose-passive or over-folding players, aggression wins. Game Arena's ranking follows aggression and refusal to fold the big blind. Chipzen's amateur rule-bot field is likely closer to that than to GTO Wizard AI, so an aggressive, value-heavy policy with fold-equity bets is the default to test first.

## Q6. Which ideas transfer to training a small non-LLM bot (0.5 vCPU, pure Python plus numpy; 20-core box plus GB10 for about 48 h offline)

### Takeaway
What transfers is the scaffolding around the LLMs, not the LLMs: solver-labelled distillation with full coverage of odd lines and stack depths, self-play with per-seat baselines and a diverse opponent pool (never a single opponent), a deterministic feature or context engine, explicit opponent statistics inside an EV formula, and offline LLM or evolutionary search that outputs plain code. Tool use and opponent-model prompting do not transfer at runtime.

### Cited Findings
* Self-play curriculum: SPIRAL found self-play kept improving where fixed-opponent training stalled or hurt, and needed per-role baselines (RAE) to stay stable. ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.24119v3))
* Opponent pool: GRPO against one LLM opponent turned three rule-bot matchups from profit to loss; the authors suggest multi-opponent training. ([ECHO-2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02192))
* Distillation from solvers: PokerBench (GTO Wizard plus WASM-Postflop labels) and SpinGPT (InstaGTO argmax-EV labels via ORPO) both gain from solver labels, and both show coverage holes: donk bets for PokerBench, 200 BB shoves for SpinGPT. ([PokerBench HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.08328v2); [SpinGPT HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22387v2))
* Reward design: SpinGPT deliberately trains on the argmax-EV label rather than maximizing an EV reward; ISO adds a learned "Strategic Reward Model" for long-run value (no numbers). ([SpinGPT HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22387v2); [ISO abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08041))
* Feature engine and rules: PokerSkill's code-only context engine plus budgets scores -132 ± 19 mbb/hand (about -13 bb/100) against GTO Wizard AI with no LLM. ([PokerSkill HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30094v1))
* Offline LLM program search with LLM-free runtime: CSRO's evolved Leduc code beat CFR+'s returns against AlwaysCall (110.3 vs 62.1) and stayed close to unexploitable (-4.4 vs CFR+). ([CSRO HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10098v1))
* Solver-algorithm tweaks from LLM search: WOP-CFR's warm start, asymmetric discounting and optimistic prediction. ([AlphaEvolve CFR HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16928v3))
* Tool use: ToolPoker's strength comes from the solver call it learns to make, and it still loses slightly to CFR+ and DeepCFR (sibling note, not re-read). ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00528))
* Failure baselines worth adding to any eval pool, with their measured cost vs a strong bot: Always Fold -64.6, Check Call -241.1, Uniform Random -284.9, All-in -380.6 bb/100. ([GTO Wizard Benchmark HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660))

### Inferences
* Recommended offline pipeline order, given 48 h: (1) build a PokerSkill-style context engine as the feature set for both rules and any small net; (2) compute solver or CFR labels over the shallow-to-medium stack depths Chipzen actually reaches as blinds rise, including off-tree bet sizes; (3) distill into a numpy MLP or lookup tables; (4) fine-tune by self-play against a population that mixes our checkpoints with rule archetypes (fold bot, calling station, maniac, all-in bot, equity-threshold bots like those on dev.fun), using per-seat baselines as in RAE; (5) add a CSRO-style exploit layer where per-opponent fold and call frequencies enter bet EV directly.
* An LLM can still help offline: writing rule archetypes for the opponent pool, or running an AlphaEvolve-like loop that mutates our Python policy against that pool with chip EV as fitness. The output is plain code, so it fits the runtime budget. Evidence that this beats hand-built code at NLHE scale does not exist yet (CodeClash, Husky gaps).
* PokerSkill's repo is CC BY-NC 4.0 per its README, so copying its rule tables into a bot that plays for prizes may conflict with the non-commercial term. Reimplementing the idea (context engine plus pressure budgets) from the paper is the safer route.
* Per-decision LLM calls, opponent-model prompting and solver tool calls are ruled out by the runtime, and none of them showed profit against strong bots anyway.

### Gaps
* I found no 2025 to 2026 work that distills LLM reasoning (as opposed to solver labels) into a small non-LLM poker policy.
* No paper tests any of these methods in a short, rising-blind, winner-take-all HU format; transfer to Chipzen is inference only.
