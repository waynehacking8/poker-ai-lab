# Fast opponent adaptation for heads-up hold'em agents: opponent models plus best-responding decision procedures (2020 to Nov 2026)

Fetch date for every source: 2026-11-21. Method: web search, abstract and HTML page fetches, and full-text reads of arXiv/PMLR/NeurIPS PDFs converted locally with pdftotext (marked "full text"). Items seen only in a search snippet or abstract are marked as such. GitHub numbers come from api.github.com on 2026-11-21. chipzen.ai was not contacted.

Scope note: the earlier project file `research_notes/HUNL bot algorithms frontier 2026/opponent_modeling_exploitation.md` already summarizes CS-RNR, SafeAdapt-Poker, Safe Active De-censoring (SAD), ABD, OX-Search, SES, StratFormer and AlphaExploitem headline numbers, Ganzfried's consistent opponent modeling, and DBR/RNR. This file adds what goes beyond that: adaptation-speed numbers (performance versus hands observed), model sizes and runtime shape, the meta-RL and in-context line (L2E, GSCU, TAO, OMIS), the implicit-modelling line, HUNL evidence with margins, and code status.

Setting used for relevance judgments: heads-up NLHE, mostly rule-based amateur bots, winner-take-all, blinds rise every 20 hands, median match 30 to 44 hands (so adaptation must land within about 10 to 30 hands), runtime 0.5 vCPU pure Python plus numpy under 200 ms per decision, offline 20 cores plus a GB10 GPU for about 48 hours.

## 1. Opponent-conditioned and meta-learned policies (meta-RL, in-context transformers, recurrent embeddings, few-shot poker adaptation): what adaptation speed, in which games?

### Takeaway
Every learned adaptive policy with published numbers was evaluated on Kuhn, Leduc or BigLeduc (or non-poker grid games), and over hundreds to thousands of hands per opponent; none reports performance as a function of hands at the 10 to 30 hand scale in hold'em. The fastest documented numbers are SafeAdapt-Poker's 8.9 to 11.9 hand recovery after an opponent shift (Leduc, with a pretrained pool prior) and OMIS's use of only the 3 most recent episodes as context (non-poker). The transferable ideas are cheap: running per-context fold/call/raise rates as network inputs (StratFormer), a tiny transformer context over the last few hands (OMIS: 3 blocks, hidden size 32), and a bandit switch between a greedy conditioned response and a fixed safe policy (GSCU).

### Cited Findings
- **L2E, "Learning to Exploit Your Opponent" (Wu, Li, Xing et al., arXiv 2102.09381, 2021; background for the 2023-2026 line).** Meta-learns a base policy (MAML-style) against automatically generated "hard-to-exploit" and MMD-diverse opponents so that adaptation to a new opponent takes 3 gradient steps at test time. Policy net [64,64]; training samples 40 opponents per batch and 20 trajectories per opponent. Full text read. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.09381)
- L2E Leduc results after the 3-step adaptation (average return): vs Random 0.42±0.32, Call 1.34±0.14, Rocks 0.38±0.17, Nash -0.03±0.14, cheating Oracle -1.15±0.27; MAML baseline 1.27, -0.23, -1.42, -0.77, -2.93; explicit opponent modeling (EOM) baseline 0.30, -0.01, -0.13, -0.36, -1.82. BigLeduc (24 cards, up to 6 raises): L2E vs Random 0.82, Call 0.74, Raise 0.68, Oracle -1.02. The paper does not state how many test hands feed each gradient step in the passage I read. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.09381)
- **GSCU, "Greedy when Sure and Conservative when Uncertain about the Opponents" (Fu et al., Tencent AI Lab, ICML 2022, PMLR 162:6829-6848).** Offline: a conditional VAE learns opponent policy embeddings ("Policy2Emb") and one embedding-conditioned best response. Online: variational Bayesian posterior over the current opponent's embedding (posterior of the last step is the prior of the next), plus EXP3 choosing between the greedy conditioned response and a fixed worst-case-optimal conservative policy; regret is provably no worse than always playing the conservative policy. Games: Kuhn poker (OpenSpiel) and Predator Prey. Full text read. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/fu22b.html), [PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/fu22b/fu22b.pdf)
- GSCU adaptation horizon: in Kuhn the opponent switches every M = 1000 episodes (hands) in the main runs (PP: 200); checkpoints are taken every 100 episodes and each is scored with 10,000 extra episodes. In the sensitivity study with M = 250, 500, 1000, performance "improves as M increases", most in the "seen" setting; in the "adaptive" (learning) opponent setting GSCU is "least exploitable (same as π1)", the conservative arm. — [PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/fu22b/fu22b.pdf)
- GSCU code: github.com/YeTianJHU/GSCU, 26 stars, MIT, last push 2022-08-04 (GitHub API); PyTorch plus OpenSpiel. — [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/YeTianJHU/GSCU)
- **TAO, "Towards Offline Opponent Modeling with In-context Learning" (Jing, Li, Liu, Zang, Fu, Fu, Xing, Cheng; ICLR 2024).** Three stages: learn policy embeddings from offline data, train a response policy on trajectory fragments, adapt to unknown opponents purely in context. Claimed equivalent to Bayesian posterior sampling over opponent policies, with convergence in opponent recognition, and "faster and superior adaptation", especially to unseen opponents. Abstract level only; environments were not confirmed as poker; no code found. — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/a1d04870cf83a0f29819d66f1dfdbfcb-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **OMIS, "Opponent Modeling with In-context Search" (Jing et al., NeurIPS 2024).** A GPT-2 decoder trained by in-context pretraining into three heads (actor, opponent imitator, critic); at test time it guesses the opponent's current policy from context and runs a decision-time search with that imagined opponent. Environments: Predator Prey, Level-Based Foraging, Overcooked; the paper states it "focuses on the setting of perfect information games", and the main experiments assume the ground-truth transition model. Full text read. — [NeurIPS abstract](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/710445227fa8c1b6a9ceada902dd4741-Abstract.html), [text mirror](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/NeurIPS/2024/opponent_modeling_with_incontext_search__c0464fde.txt)
- OMIS adaptation-speed setup and size (App. H.3): opponents switch every E = 20 episodes in the main runs (ablation E in {2, 5, 10, 20, random}); 1200 test episodes; in-context data built from only the C = 3 most recent trajectories, episode-wise sequence length H = 15, max backbone sequence 20; backbone 3 self-attention blocks, hidden size 32, 1 head; search M = 3 rollouts of length L = 3 per legal action, search discount 0.7. Returns appear only in figures; text claims "higher average return and lower variance" than DRON, LIAM, MeLIBA, Meta-PG, Meta-MAPG, MBOM and SP-MCTS. Opponent imitator action accuracy in Overcooked with seen:unseen = 10:0 is 79.8±0.3%. — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/710445227fa8c1b6a9ceada902dd4741-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- OMIS code: the paper links only a project site (sites.google.com/view/nips2024-omis). A repo github.com/Luzy2019/omis describes itself as "source code from Opponent Modeling with In-context Search" (2 stars, no license, last push 2025-12-24); its tree includes `harfang_utils`, so it looks like a third-party adaptation; official status unverified. — [NeurIPS poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/94501), [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/Luzy2019/omis)
- **StratFormer (Caen, Winands, Soemers; arXiv 2604.25796, Apr 2026), details beyond the earlier notes.** Causal transformer, 4 layers, d_model 512, 8 heads, FFN 512, dropout 0.15, sequences up to 3000 tokens ("approximately 375 hands of context"); each token carries 25 features including 15 "bucket-rate" features (fold/call/raise rates across five strategic contexts, maintained by a running opponent-statistics tracker); encoder initialized from a GTO imitation model; trained about 72 h on one Apple M-series GPU (20,000 epochs, 1,468 gradient updates). Evaluation 3,000 hands × 3 trials per opponent: positive gain vs 8 of 12 opponents (6 of 12 significant), mean +0.106 BB/hand, +0.821 vs maniac_high, +0.193 vs loose_aggressive_high, +0.173 vs maniac_mid; vs GTO itself -0.050 BB/hand. I found no curve of gain versus hands observed in the text. Full text read. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25796)
- **AlphaExploitem (Murgoci, Spaan, Oren; TU Delft; arXiv 2605.09150, 9 May 2026), details beyond the earlier notes.** Hierarchical transformer: a within-hand encoder pools each completed hand, an across-hand encoder attends over all completed hands against the current opponent; very small (Leduc: 1 layer, embedding 16, 2 heads, FFN 1024). Training: K-best league self-play plus hand-crafted exploitable "toys" (8 in-distribution, 12 held-out for Leduc), 8 seeds, about 12 h per Leduc seed on one A40. Masking the cross-hand history at evaluation drops Leduc reward from +1.10 to +0.54 (in-distribution) and +1.16 to +0.52 (held-out), so the history contributes about +0.55 to +0.64 per hand. No reward-versus-hands-observed curve and no exploitability measurement of the agent itself; checklist says "The code will be publicly released". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09150), [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09150)
- **Policy representation learning (Wang, Yang, Prakash, Greenwald; arXiv 2607.01498, 1 Jul 2026).** Self-supervised embeddings of policies in Kuhn and Leduc, with dataset construction methods and downstream evaluation tasks; abstract says "useful behavioral representations are present in the learned embeddings". No numbers on the abstract page. Code github.com/VitamintK/ssl-project (2 stars, no license, last push 2026-08-31). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01498), [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/VitamintK/ssl-project)
- **LLM in-context adaptivity (Liu, Li, Schultz; arXiv 2611.21710, 16 Nov 2026).** Repeated Leduc and NLHE against 35 unknown exploitable opponents with a paired memory/no-memory protocol. Memory gives "little performance gain for open-weight models", frontier models are inconsistent, and a "memory-doing gap" appears (models record tendencies but rarely change play). An SFT-then-trajectory-RL Qwen3.5-9B raises "experience gain" 2x over SFT in Leduc and 5x in NLHE; only relative multipliers given; no code. Not runtime-feasible here, but it is the only 2026 NLHE in-context adaptation result I found. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21710)
- TransAM, a transformer agent-modeling method, was surfaced by a search summary as cited with venue RLC 2025; which paper cites it was not confirmed and TransAM itself was not read. Unverified lead. — [search hit, arXiv 2609.34342](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34342)

### Inferences
- The only evidence for adaptation inside 10 to 30 hands comes from methods that start from a strong prior over a known pool (SafeAdapt's pretrained encoder, Ganzfried-Wang-Chiswick's Nash-centered prior in section 2) rather than from end-to-end meta-learners. Meta-learned transformers were trained and scored on hundreds to thousands of hands.
- Runtime shape (my estimate, not measured): OMIS-scale networks (3 blocks, hidden 32, sequence 20) are small enough for numpy under 200 ms even with dozens of forward passes; StratFormer-scale (4 × 512 with up to 3000 tokens) is unlikely to fit 0.5 vCPU at 200 ms without heavy truncation or caching; AlphaExploitem's Leduc sizes fit, but no HUNL-size version exists.
- The cheapest transferable piece is StratFormer's bucket-rate features: HUD-style per-context frequencies fed to a small policy, which a 48 h offline run can train against a cloned bot pool.

### Gaps
- No paper reports win rate versus number of hands observed in HUNL or limit hold'em for a learned adaptive policy; L2E, GSCU, StratFormer, AlphaExploitem stop at Kuhn/Leduc/BigLeduc.
- L2E test-time sample count per gradient step not confirmed from the text read; L2E venue not confirmed (arXiv plus an OpenReview PDF).
- TAO environments, numbers and code not checked beyond the abstract.

## 2. Bayesian and type-based opponent modeling with learned priors from population data (hierarchical priors, mixture-of-types posteriors, de-censoring hidden cards): latest 2024-2026 poker evidence

### Takeaway
The 2024-2026 poker evidence for Bayesian or type-based adaptation is small-game only: Ganzfried-Wang-Chiswick (3-player Kuhn, Nash-centered Dirichlet prior, exploitation can start immediately), GSCU's posterior over a learned embedding (Kuhn), and SAD's showdown-censoring correction (already in earlier notes). I found no 2024-2026 paper with a hierarchical prior learned from population hand histories at hold'em scale; the closest population-prior work in NLHE is still Ponsen et al. 2008 (background). The prior choice dominates in short matches: an uninformed prior turned the best agent into the second worst.

### Cited Findings
- **Ganzfried, Wang, Chiswick, "Opponent Modeling in Multiplayer Imperfect-Information Games" (arXiv 2212.06027 v4, 29 Jul 2024; DAI 2024; ACM DL DOI 10.1145/3719545.3721108).** Prior: independent Dirichlet per opponent and seat with means at exact or approximate Nash equilibria (midpoint of a robust equilibrium family), k = 10 sampled strategies per opponent; posterior by Bayes' rule on observed play, with cards seen only at showdown (the likelihood treatment of hidden cards is not detailed). — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06027v4), [DAI 2024 PDF](http://www.adai.ai/dai/2024/dai_papers/DAI2024_paper_10.pdf), [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3719545.3721108)
- Results (3-player Kuhn, 10 matches of 3,000 hands over six seatings, duplicate deals, millichips/hand): MBBR 48, best Nash agents 37 / 35 / 30, other agents from 34 down to -172; standard errors 0.4 to 0.6. With the naive uninformed prior (all Dirichlet parameters 2) MBBR scored -54, second to last. Switch times H from 0 to 200 hands gave "nearly identical" win rates (exploitation can start at once), larger H hurt roughly linearly; larger k helped but runtime grows as k^(n-1). — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06027v4)
- The same paper describes Ganzfried-Sandholm 2011 "deviation-based best response" (Nash as prior mean, closest model consistent with observations), which beat an approximate Nash strategy against several of the weakest limit hold'em ACPC agents but was not tested against strong or medium opponents (background, pre-2020). — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06027v4)
- GSCU (ICML 2022) is the clearest deep mixture-of-types posterior: variational posterior over a learned policy-embedding space instead of a discrete type, updated online, with a bandit fallback. Kuhn only. — [PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/fu22b/fu22b.pdf)
- Bayesian policy reuse line (BPR+ AAMAS 2016 for switching opponents; Bayes-ToMoP IJCAI 2019; Deep BPR+ JAAMAS 2020 with a distilled policy network and a "rectified belief model" over reward and behavior signals). A later paper on Bayes-Lab states most BPR methods assume the opponent's local information is observable during play, a poor fit for hidden cards. Search snippets only; pre-2021 except the MDPI paper (year not confirmed). — [AAMAS 2016](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2016/pdfs/p1315.pdf), [Deep BPR+](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10458-020-09480-9), [Bayes-ToMoP](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/88.md), [Bayes-Lab, MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/14/6953)
- Background population-prior work in NLHE: Ponsen, Ramon, Croonenborghs, Driessens, Tuyls (AAAI 2008) start from a population-level model of "how opponents play" and fit a relational regression tree that corrects it per player; hands with unseen cards still contribute (logged as wins or losses). Snippet level only. — [KU Leuven PDF](https://lirias2repo.kuleuven.be/bitstream/handle/123456789/183259/ponsen_aaai08.pdf;sequence=1), [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/aaai/2008/ponsen2008aaai-bayes)
- Background: Bayes' Bluff (Southey et al., UAI 2005) samples opponent strategies from a prior (independent Dirichlet for Leduc, an informed prior for Texas hold'em) and compares Bayesian best response, MAP response and Thompson's response. Snippet level. — [arXiv 1207.1411](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.1411)
- Ganzfried-Sun Bayesian opponent exploitation (arXiv 1603.03491, last revised 2018) discusses a prior that is uniform over a region around a "population mean" strategy from historical data. Snippet level, background. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.03491)
- Practitioner sample-size heuristics for HUD stats (no stated methodology): VPIP and PFR need about 20 hands before drawing conclusions; about 100 hands gives "a pretty good idea"; around 1,000 hands a player "is pretty much what his VP$IP/PFR says". — [BlackRain79](https://www.blackrain79.com/2017/11/poker-hud-stat-sample-size.html), [PokerStrategy glossary](https://www.pokerstrategy.com/glossary/VPIP/), [CardPlayer](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1035874)

### Inferences
- With 30 to 44 hands, frequency estimates are noisy: for a true VPIP of 20%, the binomial standard error is about 12.6 points at 10 hands, 8.9 at 20, 7.3 at 30, 6.3 at 40 (my calculation, independence assumed). Discriminating between a handful of archetypes is feasible in that window only if the archetypes differ by large margins; estimating a full strategy is not. That favors a posterior over a small library of types (learned offline from the logs) over a free-form model.
- The Ganzfried-Wang-Chiswick prior ablation (48 vs -54 millichips/hand) is the strongest evidence I found that the prior, not the update rule, decides short-horizon results. For our case the natural prior is the empirical population of logged bots, not Nash.
- Hidden-card handling: rule-based bots that fold often censor their holdings; SAD (earlier notes) and Ponsen-style win/loss credit are the two available treatments. Rule-based bots with deterministic thresholds make the likelihood of an action given cards nearly 0/1, which makes Bayesian updates fast when the model class is right and brittle when it is wrong (my inference).

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 hold'em paper with a hierarchical or population prior learned from logged hands, and no adaptation-speed curve for any Bayesian poker method.
- Bayes' Bluff and Ponsen 2008 numbers were not fetched.

## 3. Search against an opponent model and safe exploitation with search: how do they trade adaptation against exploitability?

### Takeaway
The safe-search line (SES 2022, OX-Search 2024, CDBR/CDRNR 2024, ABD 2025, CS-RNR and SafeAdapt-Poker 2026, plus a multiplayer certificate in Nov 2026) all assume a given or slowly estimated model and pay for safety in samples. CS-RNR states that a diffuse deviation needs on the order of 10^3 observations to confirm, and it releases exploitation against only 1 of 12 opponents in 800 hands; SafeAdapt recovers in about 9 to 12 hands after a shift but only because its encoder and risk model were calibrated offline on the opponent pool. For 30-hand matches, a statistical confirmation gate alone will mostly keep you at equilibrium; safety has to come from the offline prior and a deviation budget instead.

### Cited Findings
- **CS-RNR (Li and Huang, arXiv 2607.28520), adaptation-speed details beyond the earlier notes.** The scheduler tests pin levels p in {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9} only at checkpoints, hands {40, 80, 160, 320, 480, 640} for a T = 800 hand episode; each candidate is certified by one exact best-response pass over a restricted solve of 400 CFR+ iterations. "A pool-frequency shift from 0.1 to 0.28 needs on the order of 10^3 observations to separate from equilibrium, while 800 hands supply only tens per pool"; CS-RNR releases exploitation against 1, 5 and 7 of 12 opponents as T grows from 800 to 2,400 to 5,000. Adversarial modes: an unbudgeted system loses -1.79/hand where CS-RNR stays within tolerance; decoy-then-punish caps loss at -0.009/hand versus -1.25/hand unbudgeted; Nash-then-deviate for 400 hands delays detection. Full text read. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28520)
- **SafeAdapt-Poker (Hu et al., arXiv 2610.20473), speed details beyond the earlier notes.** Recovery time is defined as the first hand after a declared shift at which the rolling gain stays positive for a set number of consecutive hands. Table 1 (exact Leduc, fixed anchor): full SafeAdapt weak-pool gain +0.0718±0.0058 vs naive best response +0.1102, worst-opponent degradation -0.0126 vs -0.1846 (naive BR) and -0.0298 (OX-Search); snapshot exploitability 0.0264 and recovery 8.9 hands, both lowest among adaptive methods. Bait-and-switch: regret 0.0578 vs 0.0963 (OX-Search) and 0.1784 (count statistics), recovery 11.9 hands vs 22.5 and 41.7; StratFormer recovers in 16.9 to 27.4 hands; shift-detection AUROC 0.856 to 0.902. Full text read. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20473)
- **CDBR / CDRNR (Milec, Kubicek, Lisy; arXiv 2112.12594 v7, 3 Apr 2024; ALA 2024 workshop).** Depth-limited best response and depth-limited restricted Nash response using a precomputed optimal value function, so responses to a new fixed model are computed quickly. Leduc: CDBR with lookahead 3 reaches 96% of the full best-response gain against uniform random (CDBR1 74.4%). CDRNR "can achieve over half of the possible gain without almost any exploitability" and "rarely reaches even a tenth of the gain in exploitability" at p = 0.5. The model is given (fixed strategies), not learned from a sample count; no per-decision timings; no code. — [arXiv HTML v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.12594v7), [ALA 2024 PDF](https://ala2024.github.io/papers/ALA2024_paper_13.pdf)
- **ABD (AAMAS 2025), detail beyond the earlier notes:** it explicitly studies adapting to a "known sub-rational opponent" and targets real-time use ("not require more than a few seconds"); opponent modeling itself is out of scope. Full text read. — [arXiv PDF v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10464v3)
- **Safe Subgame Resolving in Multiplayer Games (Li and Huang, arXiv 2611.25981, 19 Nov 2026).** A post-hoc certificate for multiplayer subgame resolving; without it, resolving raised the best-response gap past tolerance in 12% to 100% of updates; the certificate flagged every such update and accepted 41% (Kuhn) and 86% (3-player Leduc) of standard updates; one best-response pass per opponent. Code github.com/lbn187/MultiPlayerSolver (1 star, MIT, last push 2026-11-20). Relevant only as a cheap "keep the blueprint unless certified" pattern. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25981), [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/lbn187/MultiPlayerSolver)
- Ganzfried-Sandholm safe exploitation (TEAC 2015) computes an epsilon-safe best response over the whole game with an LP each iteration, its scalability limit (per NeurIPS 2022 SES). — [NeurIPS 2022 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b12a1d1014e952e676f5d6931d03241a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **Tournament objective (Li and Huang, "ICM Out!", arXiv 2608.09586, 10 Aug 2026).** In a 3-player jam/fold tournament, pricing successor states with computed continuation values instead of ICM shifts jam frequency by 14.08% on average and gains $214.33 prize equity per hand, preferred in 2,433 of 2,838 matched units; robust to LLM and threshold opponents. Not heads-up; no code. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09586)

### Inferences
- The numbers line up into one picture: statistical confirmation of a moderate deviation costs about 10^3 observations (CS-RNR), pool-calibrated detectors recover in about 10 hands (SafeAdapt), and Nash-centered Bayesian priors can exploit from hand 0 (section 2). Under a 30 to 44 hand horizon, only the last two are usable, and both move the work offline.
- Rule-based amateur bots usually deviate strongly (for example, an action an equilibrium takes with probability 0.3 taken every time). Under that model, 5 consistent observations already have likelihood ratio about 1/0.3^5, roughly 400 (my calculation), so a confidence gate on strong, specific deviations can open within a match even though diffuse ones cannot.
- Runtime: per-decision subgame search (OX-Search, ABD, CDBR) needs a value network and a solver per decision; at 0.5 vCPU and 200 ms only precomputed responses per type, mixed by posterior, look feasible (my judgment, not measured).
- Winner-take-all with rising blinds changes the objective from chip EV to match-win probability; ICM Out shows continuation-value pricing beats a fixed heuristic objective in tournaments, but no heads-up result exists.

### Gaps
- No safe-exploitation paper reports results at a 10 to 50 hand horizon in hold'em.
- CDRNR numeric gain/exploitability pairs are only in figures; no HUNL CDRNR result.

## 4. Implicit agent modelling (Bard et al. 2013) and its successors through 2026

### Takeaway
Implicit modelling (score a precomputed portfolio of counter-strategies online, pick or mix by estimated utility) remains the only adaptive method with a documented hold'em competition-level result, and its 2022-2026 descendants keep the shape: GSCU (EXP3 over a greedy conditioned response and a safe policy), ABD (portfolio inside depth-limited search), CS-RNR (a grid of restricted-response pin levels chosen by certificate) and SafeAdapt-Poker (a finite family of frozen controllers screened on calibration sessions). For our runtime limit this family fits best, because all heavy computation is offline.

### Cited Findings
- **Bard, Johanson, Burch, Bowling, "Online Implicit Agent Modelling", AAMAS 2013, pp. 255-262.** Instead of estimating every parameter of the opponent's strategy, the agent estimates the utility of a fixed portfolio of precomputed strategies online; tested in heads-up limit Texas hold'em; the agent "would have won the heads-up limit opponent exploitation event" of the 2011 ACPC. Abstract and bibliographic page via search only; the PDF URL now redirects to a 404, so portfolio size, estimator details and margins were not verified. — [bib page](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~bowling/publications/b2hd-13aamas-implicitmodelling.html), [AMiner abstract](https://www.aminer.cn/pub/53e99b21b7602d97023ba588/online-implicit-agent-modelling), [PDF link (dead redirect on 2026-11-21)](https://www.cs.ualberta.ca/~games/poker/publications/AAMAS13-modelling.pdf)
- Bard's PhD thesis "Online Agent Modelling in Human-Scale Problems" extends the approach to several poker domains and notes the portfolio can be built offline, leaving time for expensive robust-response computation. Snippet level. — [ERA, University of Alberta](https://era.library.ualberta.ca/items/82723f7a-1a91-4613-acb5-109fe8de285c)
- The Albrecht-Stone survey treats expert-algorithm approaches as implicit modelling (each expert is optimal against some opponent) and reports this has been effective in poker variants. Snippet level. — [arXiv 1709.08071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.08071)
- GSCU (2022) applies the same pattern with two arms and a learned embedding posterior, with a regret bound no worse than the conservative arm; Kuhn only; MIT code. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/fu22b.html), [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/YeTianJHU/GSCU)
- ABD (2025) uses a strategy portfolio and "matrix-valued states" so depth-limited search can use the opponent's known errors beyond the depth limit. — [arXiv PDF v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10464v3)
- CS-RNR (2026) selects among restricted responses on a fixed p grid at hand checkpoints, each certified before deployment. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28520)
- SafeAdapt-Poker (2026) screens "a finite family of frozen controllers" on complete paired calibration sessions before held-out play. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20473)

### Inferences
- A portfolio of K precomputed responses (one per offline-clustered bot archetype plus an equilibrium anchor), chosen by posterior or by an importance-sampled utility estimate, is a dictionary lookup at runtime and matches the 0.5 vCPU limit. It also bounds downside, since the anchor stays in the portfolio.
- In 30 to 44 hands a bandit such as EXP3 barely leaves uniform exploration; GSCU's horizon was 250 to 1000 hands per opponent. A posterior over types with an informed population prior (section 2) adapts faster than a pure bandit over the same portfolio (my inference from the horizons reported).

### Gaps
- Bard 2013 margins, portfolio size and hand counts not verified (PDF unavailable).
- No 2020-2026 implicit-modelling paper evaluated in hold'em was found.

## 5. Which methods have been shown in no-limit or limit hold'em against rule-based or exploitable opponents, and with what margins?

### Takeaway
Very few. Verified hold'em margins against exploitable opponents with a known model: CDBR beats SlumBot by 1774±137 mbb/hand in HUNL (FCPA abstraction, SlumBot's strategy given). Unverified or snippet-level: Bard 2013 implicit modelling in limit (would have won ACPC 2011 exploitation), deviation-based best response in limit against weak ACPC agents, ASHE's evolved LSTM agents in HUNL against rule-based opponents, and a 2021 neuroevolution-plus-RL HUNL exploiter. 2025-2026 HUNL "adaptive" papers either report no profit metric (Yi and Yi 2025) or report only relative LLM gains (Liu et al. 2026). Every 2026 learned exploiter with numbers (StratFormer, AlphaExploitem, SafeAdapt) is Leduc or Kuhn.

### Cited Findings
- **CDBR vs SlumBot (HUNL), ALA 2024:** CDBR 1774±137 mbb/hand, local best response (LBR) 1388±150, ABR 1259 (no interval); CDBR and LBR used fold/call/pot/all-in abstraction; the opponent model is SlumBot's own fixed strategy. — [arXiv HTML v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.12594v7)
- **Implicit modelling, heads-up limit, 2013:** would have won the 2011 ACPC heads-up limit opponent exploitation event (abstract level). — [AMiner abstract](https://www.aminer.cn/pub/53e99b21b7602d97023ba588/online-implicit-agent-modelling)
- **Deviation-based best response, limit hold'em, 2011 (background):** beat an approximate Nash agent against several of the weakest ACPC agents; not tested against strong ones. — [arXiv HTML 2212.06027v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06027v4)
- **ASHE, evolved LSTM opponent models in HUNL (Li and Miikkulainen, AAAI 2017, GECCO 2018, PhD 2018; background):** against flawed opponents reported 40% to 1360% more earnings than "cutting-edge game theoretic" players; agents evolved against weak rule-based opponents were statistically tied with Slumbot. Search snippets only; numbers not verified in full text. — [AAAI 2017 PDF](https://nn.cs.utexas.edu/downloads/papers/xun.aaai17.pdf), [PhD 2018 PDF](https://nn.cs.utexas.edu/downloads/papers/li.phd18.pdf)
- **Xu, Chen, Chen, "Efficient Opponent Exploitation in No-Limit Texas Hold'em Poker: A Neuroevolutionary Method Combined with Reinforcement Learning", Electronics 10(17):2087, Aug 2021.** Results not verified (MDPI returned 403). Code github.com/jiahui-x/NE_RL: 6 stars, no license, last push 2021-07-11. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/10/17/2087), [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/jiahui-x/NE_RL)
- **Yi and Yi, "Beyond Game Theory Optimal" (arXiv 2509.23747, Sep 2025):** on full read, no opponent archetypes, no profit metric (only agreement with a heuristic "GTO-proxy"), no adaptation rule specified, no code; internal contradictions about CFR vs MCCFR. Not usable as evidence. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23747v1)
- **Liu, Li, Schultz (arXiv 2611.21710, Nov 2026):** NLHE against unseen exploitable opponents; RL-trained 9B LLM gives 5x the "experience gain" of SFT; relative numbers only. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21710)
- Small hobby repos claim exploitative HU bots (for example THETILTED1/TiltStack, GPL-3.0, 3 stars; Madhavan113/poker-rl-agent, "in-context Bayesian opponent exploitation with small transformers", 1 star, no license); no published evaluations. — [GitHub search API](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=opponent+exploitation+poker&sort=stars)

### Inferences
- Public, verified evidence that a learned in-context or meta-learned exploiter beats rule-based bots in HUNL within a few dozen hands does not exist as of 2026-11-21. Any claim for our setting must be measured in-house: replay against bots cloned from our logged matches, report match-win rate and chips per hand as a function of hand number (hands 1 to 10, 11 to 20, 21 to 40), with a paired equilibrium-anchor baseline.
- The CDBR SlumBot margin shows the size of the prize once the model is known (about 1.8 bb/hand against a static bot); the short-match question is how much of that a posterior over a type library recovers within 10 to 30 hands. That is an open empirical question, not answered by any source here.

### Gaps
- Exact margins for Bard 2013, ASHE and Xu 2021 not verified at source.
- No ACPC-style or arena results since 2018 for exploitation events; no public numbers against rule-based amateur bot fields.
- No code for CDBR/CDRNR, ABD, CS-RNR, SafeAdapt-Poker, StratFormer, TAO; AlphaExploitem code promised but not found; OMIS code only as an unverified third-party repo.
