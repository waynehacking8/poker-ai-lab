# League training, PSRO and opponent sampling for robust game agents (2019 to Nov 2026), read for heads-up poker

Scope note: all pages were fetched on 2026-11-21 unless marked otherwise. "Snippet only" means the claim came from a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened; "unverified" means background knowledge not re-checked this session. Publication dates are the paper's own (arXiv v1 or venue date).

## Q1. AlphaStar's league: the exact recipe, and what later small-compute leagues kept

### Takeaway
AlphaStar's league is three roles that differ only in opponent distribution, snapshot rule and reset rule: main agents (35% self-play, 50% PFSP, 15% "forgotten/exploiter" PFSP, never reset), main exploiters (target current main agents, reset every time they win 70% or time out) and league exploiters (PFSP over everything, 25% reset chance). Every cheaper successor kept the exploiter-plus-reset idea and PFSP, cut the agent count to 1 main agent plus 3 to 8 exploiters, and found the biggest gains came from making exploiters diverse (strategy-conditioned or strategy-specific initializations) and from anchoring the learner with a KL term, not from more league members.

### Cited Findings
AlphaStar (Vinyals et al., Nature, 30 Oct 2019; DeepMind-hosted unformatted manuscript, text extracted with pdftotext: [AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf))
- League size: three main agents (one per race), three main exploiters (one per race), six league exploiters (two per race); each agent trained on 32 third-generation TPUs over 44 days; almost 900 distinct players created ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- PFSP: the frozen opponent B is sampled with probability proportional to f(P[A beats B]). f_hard(x) = (1 − x)^p, with p controlling entropy; f_hard(1) = 0 so no games go to opponents already beaten. The paper calls f_hard "a form of smooth approximation of max-min optimisation, as opposed to max-avg that FSP imposes", and says it matters because exploits "are strong but rare counter strategies" that a uniform mixture would ignore. f_var(x) = x(1 − x) targets opponents near the agent's level and is used "for main exploiters and struggling main agents" ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- Main agents: 35% self-play, 50% PFSP against all past players, 15% PFSP against "forgotten main players the agent can no longer beat and past main exploiters"; if none exist the 15% goes to self-play. A copy is added to the league every 2 × 10^9 steps. Main agents never reset ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- League exploiters: trained with PFSP; a frozen copy is added when they beat all league players in more than 70% of games or after 2 × 10^9 steps; at that point there is a 25% probability of reset to the supervised parameters. Purpose: find "global blind spots in the League" ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- Main exploiters: play the current main agents; "half of the time, and if the current probability of winning is lower than 20%", they use PFSP with f_var over players created by the main agents as a curriculum. Added when all three main agents are beaten in more than 70% of games or after 4 × 10^9 steps, then always reset to the supervised parameters ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- Ablations (simplified setup: Protoss vs Protoss, one map, 10^10 steps, main agents only, 50/50 self-play and PFSP unless stated). League composition: main agents only, then + main exploiters, then + league exploiters gives test Elo 1540, 1693, 1824 and relative population performance (RPP, an exploitability measure) 6%, 35%, 62%. Multi-agent algorithm: PFSP+SP Elo 1540 with min win-rate against past versions 71%; plain SP Elo 1519 but min win-rate 46%; PFSP alone and FSP had lower Elo (1273 and 1143 read in figure order). The figure text was garbled in extraction, so the number-to-label mapping is inferred from the figure order, not confirmed. The caption says "Naive self-play has high Elo, but is more forgetful", and Extended Data Fig. 5 says "PFSP based training outperforms FSP under all measures considered" ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- Robustness was validated against 17 "validation agents": strategies trained with main agents only and z fixed to hand-curated strategies such as a cannon rush; internal Elo included the built-in bots and was anchored by a bot that never acts ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- The open DI-engine league code mirrors the same constants: MainPlayer branches 0.5 PFSP / 0.35 self-play / 0.15 verification with `weighting='squared'`, snapshot every 2e9 steps, never mutates; MainExploiter snapshots on beating all 3 main players in over 70% or a 4e9-step timeout and always resets; LeagueExploiter is PFSP over all historical players with a 2e9 timeout and `mutate_prob` 0.25 ([DI-engine starcraft_player.py docs](https://di-engine-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/ding/league/starcraft_player.html), fetched 2026-11-21). "Squared" means p = 2 in f_hard.

TStarBot-X (Han et al., Tencent, arXiv 2011.13729, Nov 2020; [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729))
- Total compute 144 V100 GPUs and 13,440 CPU cores; formal run 57 days (96 GPUs training, 48 inference). League totals "about 1/30 and 1/73" of AlphaStar's data consumption and generation; model 20M parameters vs 139M; Zerg vs Zerg on one map only ([TStarBot-X](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729), 2020).
- Baseline "AlphaStar Surrogate" (1 main agent, 2 main exploiters, 2 league exploiters) failed: the main agent "converged to early Zerg rush or push strategies within days" with little variety ([TStarBot-X](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729), 2020).
- Fixes: Diversified League Training with Specific Exploiters initialized from one of K = 6 fine-tuned supervised models (each a distinct strategy: Hydralisk, Lurker, Mutalisk, Nydus, TunnelingClaws, WorkerRush) and Evolutionary / Adaptive Evolutionary Exploiters. The AEE restarts from a past exploiter whose win-rate against the main agent is 20% to 50%, preferring the one closest to 50%; otherwise it resets to its supervised model. Formal league: 1 main agent and 8 exploiters ([TStarBot-X](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729), 2020).
- Main agent mixture changed to 25% self-play, 60% PFSP, 15% PFSP against forgotten main players; main agent snapshot every 3 × 10^8 steps (about 12 h); exploiter periods 7.65 × 10^7 to 1.53 × 10^8 steps; both exploiter types reset at a step cap or above 70% win-rate ([TStarBot-X](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729), 2020).
- Rule-Guided Policy Search adds a KL term toward hand-written expert actions on six critical states (main agent only): "a slight RPP gain". DAPO (agent-level KL to the policy from the previous ~12 h period, applied only in the first 4 minutes of each game, last 15 of 57 days) gave the largest RPP gain; the authors write "DAPO is necessary to promote policy improvement". Diversified exploiters raised main-agent RPP clearly while league-wide RPP rose only slightly ([TStarBot-X](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729), 2020).

ROA-Star (Huang et al., Tencent, NeurIPS 2023; [paper PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf), text extracted with pdftotext)
- League: one main agent, one main exploiter, two league exploiters (4 learners). Main agent snapshot every 2 × 10^8 steps; league exploiter adds a copy at >70% against the whole league or a 2 × 10^8 timeout, then resets with 25% probability; main exploiter adds a copy and resets at >70% against the main agent or after 4 × 10^8 steps. The authors say "the league setting is almost the same with AlphaStar" apart from the exploiter changes ([ROA-Star](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf), 2023).
- Diagnosis: in their AlphaStar re-implementation, "the exploiters tend to lose the ability to identify the weaknesses in the main agent" in later iterations ([ROA-Star](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf), 2023).
- Fix: goal-conditioned exploiters. At each reset an exploiter becomes one of three kinds: 20% unconditional (AlphaStar style), 30% Exploitative Exploiter (reset to the current main agent, conditioned on a strategy z from the top 10% by main-agent win rate, z fixed until the next reset), 50% Explorative Exploiter (reset to the supervised model, conditioned on the top 15% of z by execution deviation). The strategy set D has 193 z values obtained by clustering human build orders and sampling clusters equally. A policy distillation loss pulls each agent toward its last reset target ([ROA-Star](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf), 2023).
- Results after 10 days vs a same-compute AlphaStar replica (round robin, 100 matches per pair): main agent worst-case win rate 44% vs 1%, average win rate 84.1% vs 70.2%, RPP 0.7843 vs 0.2157. Opponent modeling lifted win rates against fixed single-strategy opponents, e.g. VC Stalker play 22% → 52%, Void Ray push 54% → 60% ([ROA-Star](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf), 2023).
- Compute per training agent: 64 V100 GPUs and 4,600 CPU cores, 2,400 concurrent games, vs AlphaStar's 256 TPU cores, 4,100 CPU cores and 16,000 games; 50-day league produced 768 models; ablations ran 5 days each ([ROA-Star](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf), 2023).

SCC (Wang et al., ICML 2021; [ar5iv 2012.13169](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.13169), [PMLR v139](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wang21v.html))
- "Agent branching": a new agent is initialized from the current main agent (not the supervised model) and given a dense reward for a chosen strategy z; z-conditioned branches scored 35% to 65% against the unconditional main agent; a branched main agent reached the needed level after about 15 days. Claimed "order of magnitude less computation" than AlphaStar (about 1,000 concurrent matches per agent vs 16,000), but no ablation isolates branching's savings and no total hardware figure is given ([SCC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.13169), 2021).

Minimax Exploiter (Bairamian et al., Ubisoft La Forge, AAMAS 2024; [arXiv 2311.17190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17190))
- Replaces the exploiter's win/loss reward with a reward that minimizes the opponent's maximum Q-value at each step, aimed at league training that is affordable in game production; tested on Tic-Tac-Toe, Connect 4, Atari Boxing and For Honor; authors claim better stability, data efficiency and faster convergence than standard exploiters (snippet only; full paper not opened) ([arXiv 2311.17190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17190), Nov 2023).

Poker league practice
- AlphaHoldem (Zhao et al., AAAI-22): end-to-end self-play RL for HUNL "using only one PC with three days training", 2.9 ms per decision on one GPU, beat Slumbot and DeepStack over 100,000 hands; includes a new metric "for evaluating and selecting the final model" ([AAAI paper page](https://aaai.org/papers/04689-alphaholdem-high-performance-artificial-intelligence-for-heads-up-no-limit-poker-via-end-to-end-reinforcement-learning/), page lists 1 Feb 2023 although the conference was AAAI-22). Its opponent scheme, K-best self-play, "always selects the top K agents, based on their ELO ratings" to generate samples (snippet of the [2024 self-play survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.01072)); OpenHoldem describes it as self-play "among the main agent and its K best historical versions" (snippet of [OpenHoldem, arXiv 2012.06168](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.06168)). The AAAI full text was blocked (bot protection), so K and hardware counts are unverified.

### Inferences
- What every small-compute league kept: (1) at least one exploiter that is reset whenever it wins about 70% or times out, (2) PFSP with f_hard so the learner focuses on whoever still beats it, (3) a mix of current self-play and past snapshots so the learner does not forget. What they cut: agents per role (4 learners in ROA-Star, 1 main agent in TStarBot-X and ROA-Star).
- What they found matters most when compute is short: diversity of exploiter starting points or goals (TStarBot-X specific exploiters, ROA-Star 20/30/50 reset mix, SCC branching from main) and a KL anchor (TStarBot-X DAPO, ROA-Star distillation to the reset target). Plain AlphaStar-style unconditioned exploiters at small scale collapsed into one rush strategy (TStarBot-X) or stopped finding holes (ROA-Star).
- Poker analogue: the "strategy statistic z" maps naturally onto bot archetypes (open-shove frequency, limp rate, fold-to-raise, sizing), so ROA-Star's exploitative and explorative exploiters translate into exploiters conditioned on an archetype vector. This is my mapping, not a published poker result.

### Gaps
- The Nature supplementary pseudocode (official `league.py`) was not opened; the recipe above is from the Methods text plus DI-engine's re-implementation. DI-engine's `_strong_win_rate` and `min_valid_win_rate` values were not visible in the fetched excerpt.
- The AlphaStar ablation figure numbers were extracted from a garbled PDF text layer; the exact label for each Elo and min-win-rate value should be checked against the figure image.
- No 2024 to 2026 league paper for StarCraft beyond ROA-Star was found in this pass; DI-star (code only) was not analysed.
- AlphaHoldem's K, pool update rule and hardware were not confirmed from the primary paper.

## Q2. PSRO variants 2020 to 2026: which reached poker, and with what results

### Takeaway
Nearly every PSRO variant reports poker results only on Leduc (sometimes Kuhn), mostly as exploitability curves in figures; none of the papers found reports PSRO results on limit or no-limit hold'em. The strongest 2026 evidence cuts against PSRO as a learner: an ICLR 2026 study over 7,000 runs found original PSRO "largely noncompetitive" with tuned PPO/MMD, and a 2026 DeepMind paper measured PSRO-IMPALA losing badly on population metrics in repeated Leduc. PSRO's useful parts for us are its meta-solvers (no-regret mixtures over a population) and its bookkeeping, not its per-iteration best-response training.

### Cited Findings
- Pipeline PSRO (McAleer, Lanier, Fox, Baldi; arXiv 2006.08555, 15 Jun 2020): trains best responses in a hierarchical pipeline of parallel workers; the abstract says DCH and Rectified PSRO "fail to converge even in small games", P2SRO converges faster with more workers, and it "beats all existing bots" in Barrage Stratego ([arXiv 2006.08555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08555)). Leduc exploitability numbers were not in the abstract. NeurIPS 2020 venue is unverified. Code: JBLanier/pipeline-psro, 58 stars, MIT, last push 2024-08-30 ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/JBLanier/pipeline-psro), 2026-11-21).
- XDO (McAleer et al., arXiv 2103.06426): double oracle for extensive-form games (title from search only, [arXiv 2103.06426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.06426)). Code indylab/nxdo, 41 stars, MIT, last push 2021-08-27 ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/indylab/nxdo), 2026-11-21).
- Anytime PSRO (McAleer et al., arXiv 2201.07700, Jan 2022): restricts only the learner to the population, lets the opponent best-respond in the full game, and learns the restricted mixture with a no-regret algorithm against that best response; on Leduc and random normal-form games it reaches "far lower exploitability than DO and PSRO" and decreases monotonically (snippet of abstract, [arXiv 2201.07700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07700)). APSRO "does not increase exploitability from one iteration to the next" up to approximation error ([SP-PSRO ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.06541)).
- Self-Play PSRO (McAleer et al., arXiv 2207.06541; ICLR 2024 as "Toward Optimal Policy Population Growth in Two-Player Zero-Sum Games"): each iteration adds two strategies per player, the best response plus the time-average of a new strategy trained off-policy as a best response to the opponent's best response; meta-distribution by regret minimization (Exp3 tabular; Multiplicative Weights, learning rate 0.1, in neural runs). On Leduc it shows "a drastic improvement in performance starting in the first iteration" over APSRO and PSRO, values only in a figure. Compute: a 128-core, 4×RTX 3090 box; neural runs used at most 14 cores and under 5 GB VRAM; runs took 1 to 7 days. Neural PSRO baseline meta-solver: 2,000 iterations of fictitious play on a payoff matrix estimated from 3,000 rollouts per matchup. Limitation: SP-PSRO can plateau above APSRO, and the authors switch to APSRO after some iterations with no principled rule ([SP-PSRO ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.06541); [ICLR 2024 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/a6ab94e907d24814e3a3ceda39a2ec64-Paper-Conference.pdf)).
- NeuPL (Liu et al., arXiv 2202.07415, 2022) and Simplex-NeuPL (Liu, Lanctot, Marris, Heess, ICML 2022): one conditional network represents the whole population; Simplex-NeuPL also learns best responses to any mixture on the population simplex and reports near-optimal returns against arbitrary mixtures in a game with tractable best responses. NeuPL's experiments were rock-paper-scissors, running-with-scissors and MuJoCo Football; no poker experiments were found (search summaries of [NeuPL](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2202.07415) and [Simplex-NeuPL, PMLR v162](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/liu22h.html); the Simplex-NeuPL experiments section was not opened).
- Policy Space Diversity PSRO (PSD-PSRO, arXiv 2306.16884, 2023) is the diversity-regularized baseline used by later Leduc papers (title only, [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2306.16884v2); venue unverified).
- Fusion-PSRO (arXiv 2405.21027, v6 dated 5 Jan 2026): initializes each new best response by averaging the parameters of population policies weighted by the meta-Nash probabilities (from iteration c = 2 on Leduc), then trains it against meta-Nash opponents. Leduc exploitability at iteration 150: vanilla PSD-PSRO 0.538, fusing top-1 0.469, top-2 0.426, top-4 0.406, all policies 0.377 ([arXiv 2405.21027v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.21027v6)). Conflict: a search snippet of the same paper reported PSRO 0.626, PSD-PSRO 0.559, Fusion-PSRO 0.497, Fusion-PSD-PSRO 0.427, probably from an earlier version (snippet only).
- Conflux-PSRO (arXiv 2410.22776, late 2024): per-state selection among population policies to improve the best response; claims significantly lower Leduc exploitability (snippet only, [arXiv 2410.22776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.22776)).
- Simulation-Free PSRO (Liu et al., arXiv 2601.05279, submitted 30 Dec 2025): identifies "game simulation is the primary bottleneck" in PSRO runtime and proposes a Dynamic Window, a bounded population where Nash Clustering picks which strategy to evict ([arXiv 2601.05279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05279)). The claim that it slightly beats PSD-PSRO on Leduc is snippet only.
- Global PSRO (Zhang et al., Tsinghua, arXiv 2605.28273, 27 May 2026): trains several candidate responses with parameter-shared conditional networks and keeps the one that minimizes the population exploitability (PE, lowest exploitability of any mixture over the population) after expansion; PPO oracles in OpenSpiel; reports lower PE than PSRO with Nash, AlphaRank, PRD, Uniform and Anytime PSRO on Liar's Dice, Leduc and Goofspiel (Leduc values in figures only). Goofspiel-13 at about 153.6M steps: 0.046 ± 0.016 vs 0.193 ± 0.044 (PSRO-Nash) and 0.191 ± 0.061 (APSRO) ([arXiv 2605.28273v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28273v1)).
- Code-Space Response Oracles (Hennes, Li, Schultz, Lanctot; arXiv 2603.10098, 10 Mar 2026, AAMAS extended abstract): PSRO with an LLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) writing each best response as code, K = 20 iterations. Repeated Leduc, 100 hands per match, evaluated against a fixed population {CFR+ Nash, AlwaysCall, AlwaysFold}. PopReturn / PopExpl / AggScore: CSRO-AlphaEvolve 49.3 / 4.4 / 44.9; CFR+ 39.8 / 0.0 / 39.8; PSRO-IMPALA 13.3 / 58.4 / −45.0 (3 seeds). In repeated RPS against 43 bots PSRO-IMPALA scored PopReturn −108.9 and AggScore −532.1 ([arXiv 2603.10098v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10098v1)).
- Reevaluating Policy Gradient Methods for Imperfect-Information Games (Rudolph, Lichtlé, Mohammadpour, Bayen, Kolter, Zhang, Farina, Vinitsky, Sokota; ICLR 2026): over 7,000 runs, 350 hyperparameter configurations and over 345,000 CPU hours on five games with exact exploitability (2D5F Liar's Dice, 3×3 Dark Hex, abrupt Dark Hex, Phantom TTT, abrupt Phantom TTT), "FP-, DO-, and CFR-based approaches fail to outperform generic policy gradient methods"; "PSRO was largely noncompetitive", with only sporadic success in Dark Hex. They note effective PG settings use "more entropy regularization than is typically used for PG methods in single-agent problems". Limits stated by the authors: only original PSRO, evaluation at 10M steps, and all algorithms still improving past 10M ([ICLR 2026 PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2026/iclr26_reevaluating/iclr26_reevaluating.pdf)). Poker appears only as a reproduction check of OpenSpiel's NFSP Leduc numbers.
- Earlier PSRO evidence relevant to fixed bots: Lanctot et al. 2017 found in Leduc that PSRO/DCH trade some convergence precision for better results against a fixed set of bots, acting like a robust counter-strategy, while NFSP behaves more like a safe equilibrium (snippet of [arXiv 1711.00832](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.00832); pre-2019 background).
- Multi-player hold'em: a 2025 IEEE paper on diversity-enhanced hierarchical PSRO evaluated in multi-player Texas hold'em using training return, policy diversity and Nash entropy, not exploitability (snippet only, [IEEE 11437976](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11437976)).
- OpenSpiel `psro_v2` (verified through the GitHub contents API, 2026-11-21): meta-solvers in `META_STRATEGY_METHODS` are `uniform_biased`, `uniform`, `nash`, `prd` and `rm` (regret matching); training-strategy selectors include rectified, probabilistic, top-k, uniform, exhaustive and functional_probabilistic; α-Rank lives in `open_spiel/python/egt/alpharank.py` ([meta_strategies.py](https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel/blob/master/open_spiel/python/algorithms/psro_v2/meta_strategies.py); [psro_v2 folder](https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel/tree/master/open_spiel/python/algorithms/psro_v2)). Repo: 5,601 stars, Apache-2.0, last push 2026-11-14 ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/google-deepmind/open_spiel)). MALib (PSRO-oriented framework): 557 stars, MIT, last push 2023-12-14, so dormant ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/sjtu-marl/malib)).

### Inferences
- For a 48-hour budget, the PSRO outer loop (train a full best response per iteration, then refill an N×N payoff table) is the expensive part; Simulation-Free PSRO's authors say simulation dominates runtime. With 23 fixed opponents that never change, the payoff table against them only needs one row per new snapshot (23 cells), which is cheap.
- The meta-solver idea that transfers best is the no-regret restricted mixture from Anytime/Self-Play PSRO: run Hedge or regret matching over "which opponent to train against" using per-opponent losses. That is the same max-min pressure as PFSP f_hard, but with a regret guarantee.
- Do not expect published Leduc exploitability numbers to predict HUNL behavior against amateur bots; none of the variants was tested on hold'em in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No paper found with PSRO, NeuPL, or any listed variant evaluated on limit or no-limit hold'em with exploitability or win rate. "Regret-matched PSRO" exists as OpenSpiel's `rm` meta-solver, but no dedicated 2024 to 2026 poker paper on it was found.
- Anytime PSRO, PSD-PSRO, Conflux-PSRO and Simulation-Free PSRO numeric Leduc values were not retrieved (figures only or snippet only).
- XDO and Pipeline PSRO venues and Leduc numbers are unverified this session.

## Q3. Fixed external populations of non-learning bots mixed with learned opponents

### Takeaway
The closest published match to our setting is AlphaExploitem (May 2026): an AlphaHoldem-style learner trained on a mixed pool of hand-written archetype bots, a K-best Elo league of its own snapshots and a long-tail buffer of past checkpoints, sampled uniformly; it more than doubled per-hand EV against archetypes in Leduc versus league-only training, generalized to held-out archetypes, and the authors claim no loss against Nash opponents (figure-based). Large-game leagues handle fixed strategies by conditioning exploiters on human strategy statistics (ROA-Star) or by adding frozen strategy-specific agents (TStarBot-X); fixed-bot benchmarks (RRPS, repeated Leduc) report average and worst-case return together.

### Cited Findings
- AlphaExploitem (Murgoci, Spaan, Oren; arXiv 2605.09150, 9 May 2026): Kuhn and Leduc only (no HUNL). Opponent pool = hand-crafted stationary "toy" archetypes (Leduc in-distribution: maniac, lag, random, calling_station, folder, aggfish, rock, nit; 12 held-out "novel combinations or perturbations" for OOD) + K-best Elo league (8 members Kuhn, 5 Leduc) + long-tail buffer of 25 past checkpoints (Leduc). Each epoch collects trajectories from all three pools and minibatches are drawn uniformly from the combined pool; toys sampled uniformly; 8 environments per opponent (Leduc). One NVIDIA A40, 12 h per Leduc seed, 8 seeds ([arXiv 2605.09150 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09150)).
- AlphaExploitem results: Leduc in-distribution about +1.0 BB/hand vs about +0.5 for the AlphaHoldem-style league-only baseline ("more than twice the per-hand EV"); "better rewards on all toys", largest gains against maniac, LAG and OOD bluffers. Removing cross-hand history (masking) halved the gain: Leduc +1.10 / +1.16 (ID/OOD) unmasked vs +0.54 / +0.52 masked. A Nash policy earns +0.4066 (ID) and +0.5349 (OOD) against the same Leduc pools. The no-loss-vs-NE claim rests on figures, and the authors concede "None of the two models reaches exact NE"; the masked policy sat above the NE line against the pools, which the authors read as more exploitative and possibly overfit ([arXiv 2605.09150 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09150)).
- AlphaStar used fixed populations twice: RL ablations measured a best response "against a uniform mixed strategy composed of the first 10 League exploiter Protoss agents", and robustness was validated against 17 frozen hand-curated-strategy agents ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- TStarBot-X injected fixed strategy knowledge two ways: specific exploiters initialized from six strategy-specific supervised models, and rule-guided KL toward hand-written expert actions on six trigger states (main agent only) ([TStarBot-X](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729), 2020).
- ROA-Star conditioned exploiters on 193 strategy statistics clustered from human replays, plus an opponent-prediction model and scouting reward; against fixed single-strategy opponents the opponent-modeling agent improved (e.g. 22% → 52% vs VC Stalker play) ([ROA-Star](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf), 2023).
- Repeated RPS benchmark (Lanctot et al., TMLR, accepted Oct 2023): a fixed population of 43 hand-coded tournament bots (25 entrants, 18 seed bots, from randbot and rockbot up to iocainebot), shipped in OpenSpiel; RL, online-learning and LLM agents "learn good counter-strategies and generalize well, but ultimately lose to the top-performing bots" ([arXiv 2303.03196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03196); bot counts from search snippet of the [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.03196v2.pdf)).
- In that benchmark family, an agent can post high population return while one bot exploits it badly, which is why AggScore subtracts the worst case (snippet of [ALA 2024 paper](https://ala2024.github.io/papers/ALA2024_paper_05.pdf)). In repeated RPS the contextual regret-minimization baseline ContRM had PopReturn 164.8 with PopExpl 16.3, while the Gemma 3 27B LLM agent had higher PopReturn (193.2) but much worse PopExpl (67.2) ([CSRO Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10098v1), 2026).
- Safe exploitation line, adjacent to this topic: Ganzfried and Sandholm characterize strategies that exploit weak opponents without risking more than the game value ([ACM, 2015](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2716322), pre-2019). 2026 arXiv work continues it with confidence-sequence certified exploitation against fixed opponents ([arXiv 2607.28520](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28520v1), snippet only) and budgeted deviation from an anchor strategy under opponent shift ([SafeAdapt-Poker, arXiv 2610.20473](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20473), snippet only).

### Inferences
- Treat the 23 opponents as frozen league members (like AlphaStar's historical players) and sample them with PFSP f_hard on the current per-opponent match-loss rate. That directly optimizes "beat every one", because f_hard is the smooth max-min the AlphaStar authors describe. AlphaExploitem's uniform sampling is the max-avg alternative and is the wrong choice for a per-opponent floor.
- Keep a learned share (current self-play plus K-best and long-tail snapshots) in the mix so the policy does not overfit to the 23 bots. AlphaExploitem's league-plus-toys combination is the published evidence that this keeps Nash-level play roughly intact; ROA-Star's worst-case gain is the large-game evidence that diverse exploiters make the main agent robust.
- The 10 per-bot clones fitted to replays are noisy copies of real opponents. Training against perturbed variants (AlphaExploitem's OOD construction, "novel combinations or perturbations") is a cheap guard against overfitting a clone's quirks.
- AlphaExploitem's masking ablation says cross-hand context is worth about half the exploitation gain in Leduc. Our matches are 30 to 44 hands, so in-match adaptation has little data; most of the edge must come from a strong prior against the archetype mix.

### Gaps
- No paper found that trains a best response to a fixed bot population with an explicit per-opponent win-rate floor as the objective in poker. Minimax-Bayes RL and Restricted Nash Response style methods were not searched in this pass.
- AlphaExploitem's per-toy numbers and exact mixing proportions between pools are not given in the text; no HUNL or tournament-format results exist there.
- The ICML 2024 "Safe and Robust Subgame Exploitation" paper was seen only as a citation; not opened.

## Q4. Evaluation protocols for "beat every opponent in the pool"

### Takeaway
The metric that matches our gate is the worst case: minimum per-opponent win rate (ROA-Star's "worst-case win rate", the PopExpl term of AggScore, AlphaStar's "min win-rate vs past"). Elo and average win rate are misleading in non-transitive pools; RPP and population exploitability compare whole populations; Soft Condorcet Optimization is the newest ranking method. With 23 opponents a per-opponent confidence bound needs on the order of 100 to 200 matches each.

### Cited Findings
- AlphaStar used three metrics: Elo (internal league Elo with the built-in bots added, and test Elo against fixed "Ablation Test Agents"), RPP (Nash-based outcome between two populations, described as measuring exploitability), and the minimum win rate against all past versions averaged over time as a forgetting proxy; naive self-play had high Elo but low min win rate (46%) ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf), 2019).
- ROA-Star defined robustness as worst-case and average win rate of the main agent against all models of both leagues (round robin, 100 matches per pair), plus RPP(A) = P_Aᵀ M_AB P_B with P_A, P_B the Nash mixtures of the cross-league payoff matrix, citing Balduzzi et al. 2019 ([ROA-Star](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf), 2023).
- PopReturn = mean utility over the bot population; PopExpl = negative of the worst-case utility over the population; AggScore = PopReturn − PopExpl ([CSRO](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10098v1), Mar 2026, following Lanctot et al. 2023).
- Population exploitability (PE) = lowest exploitability of any mixture supported on the population, used by Global PSRO as its selection criterion ([arXiv 2605.28273v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28273v1), May 2026).
- Soft Condorcet Optimization (Lanctot et al., arXiv 2411.00119, Oct 2024; AAMAS 2025; IJCAI 2026 sister-conference extended abstract): SCO ratings are maximal for Condorcet winners when they exist, "which we show is not necessarily true for the classical rating system Elo"; within 0 to 0.043 normalized Kendall-tau of Kemeny-Young on 865 PrefLib profiles; best among baselines once 59% or more of the data is missing ([arXiv 2411.00119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00119); [IJCAI 2026](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/925)). Snippet discrepancy: 31,049 vs 31,094 Diplomacy games between versions.
- The ICLR 2026 study warns that head-to-head comparisons are intransitive, which "increases the complexity of tuning", and it releases exact exploitability computations for five large games so methods can be compared without relying on head-to-head results ([ICLR 2026 PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2026/iclr26_reevaluating/iclr26_reevaluating.pdf)).
- α-Rank (Omidshafiei et al. 2019) and Nash averaging (Balduzzi et al. 2018) are the standard game-theoretic rankings for non-transitive pools (unverified background; papers not opened this session). OpenSpiel ships `alpharank.py` (verified file exists, [GitHub](https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel/tree/master/open_spiel/python/egt)).
- Sample size for the 80% gate (my calculation with the Wilson score interval, one-sided): to show a lower bound ≥ 80% against one opponent at 95% confidence you need about 174 matches if the observed rate is 85%, 68 at 88%, 44 at 90%, 31 at 92%, 20 at 95%. Holding all 23 opponents simultaneously (Bonferroni, α = 0.05/23, z ≈ 2.85): 521, 204, 131, 91 and 58 matches per opponent respectively. Computed in Python, not from a source.

### Inferences
- Gate metric: min over the 23 opponents of the per-opponent Wilson lower bound on match-win rate. Report average win rate and an Elo or SCO ranking only as context.
- Training-time selection: keep the snapshot with the best minimum per-opponent win rate (AlphaStar's min win-rate idea), not the best average; PFSP f_hard weights double as a live dashboard of the weakest matchups.
- Add an "unseen" check: hold out a few perturbed clones or archetype variants from training, in the spirit of AlphaStar's validation agents and AlphaExploitem's OOD toys, to see whether the floor survives opponents not trained against.

### Gaps
- No source found that uses a per-opponent minimum win rate as the explicit training objective with guarantees in poker; AlphaStar's f_hard is described as a smooth approximation only.
- Nash averaging and α-Rank primary papers not re-read this session.

## Q5. What works at small compute, and a concrete recipe for our setting

### Takeaway
Published small-compute successes are single-machine poker RL (AlphaHoldem: one PC, three days, HUNL; AlphaExploitem: one A40, 12 h on Leduc) and leagues that cut the agent count to about four learners. For our gate the cheapest credible design is a PPO learner with high entropy, a frozen-opponent pool of the 23 bots sampled by PFSP f_hard, a small share of self-play and snapshot play, one periodically reset exploiter, and a per-opponent Wilson-bound gate. Full PSRO is not justified at 48 hours given the 2026 evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Compute points: AlphaStar 32 TPUv3 per agent for 44 days ([AlphaStar manuscript](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf)); TStarBot-X 144 V100 and 13,440 CPU cores for 57 days, about 1/30 to 1/73 of AlphaStar ([TStarBot-X](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729)); ROA-Star 64 V100 and 4,600 cores per agent, 50 days ([ROA-Star](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/94796017d01c5a171bdac520c199d9ed-Paper-Conference.pdf)); AlphaHoldem one PC, three days, HUNL ([AAAI page](https://aaai.org/papers/04689-alphaholdem-high-performance-artificial-intelligence-for-heads-up-no-limit-poker-via-end-to-end-reinforcement-learning/)); AlphaExploitem one A40, 12 h per Leduc seed ([arXiv 2605.09150](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09150)); SP-PSRO neural runs at most 14 cores, 1 to 7 days ([SP-PSRO](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.06541)); ICLR 2026 study 345,000 CPU hours over 7,000 runs of 10M steps, about 49 CPU hours per run (my division) ([ICLR 2026](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2026/iclr26_reevaluating/iclr26_reevaluating.pdf)).
- Generic PG with strong entropy regularization matched or beat PSRO, NFSP, ESCHER and R-NaD on exploitability at 10M steps ([ICLR 2026](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2026/iclr26_reevaluating/iclr26_reevaluating.pdf)).
- Reset thresholds that survived across AlphaStar, TStarBot-X and ROA-Star: add-and-reset an exploiter at a 70% win rate or a step timeout; league exploiters reset with 25% probability; TStarBot-X's adaptive exploiter restarts from an ancestor with 20% to 50% win rate against the main agent (sources above).
- A KL anchor to a reference policy (DAPO to the previous period's policy; ROA-Star distillation to the reset target) was the largest single robustness gain in TStarBot-X ([TStarBot-X](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729)).
- Open code: DI-engine league module (3,645 stars, Apache-2.0, last push 2025-12-07), DI-star (1,406 stars, Apache-2.0, last push 2025-03-13), mini-AlphaStar (371 stars, Apache-2.0, last push 2026-11-18), TLeague project page (151 stars, no license, last push 2024-12-09) ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/opendilab/DI-engine), [DI-star](https://api.github.com/repos/opendilab/DI-star), [mini-AlphaStar](https://api.github.com/repos/liuruoze/mini-AlphaStar), [tleague_projpage](https://api.github.com/repos/tencent-ailab/tleague_projpage), all 2026-11-21). No license on TLeague's page means its code cannot be reused without permission.

### Inferences (proposed recipe, not a published result; every number below is a starting point to tune)
1. Objective and reward: train on match win/loss under the real format (10,000 stack, blinds up every 20 hands, winner takes all), not chips per hand, because short rising-blind matches make the two diverge. Add chip EV only as an auxiliary or shaped term.
2. Learner: one main agent, PPO with entropy regularization higher than single-agent defaults (ICLR 2026), plus a KL anchor to the previous snapshot (TStarBot-X DAPO analogue). Distil or export to a small numpy MLP for the 0.5 vCPU runtime.
3. Opponent mixture for the main agent (AlphaStar 35/50/15 adapted): about 55% PFSP f_hard (p = 2, squared, as in DI-engine) over the 23 frozen bots plus perturbed variants of the 10 clones; about 20% K-best self-play (K around 5, as in AlphaExploitem's Leduc league); about 10% PFSP over a long-tail snapshot buffer (around 25 entries); about 15% against the current exploiter. If the minimum per-opponent win rate is below the gate, shift share from self-play to the f_hard bot pool.
4. Exploiters: one main exploiter that trains only against the main agent, f_var curriculum when it wins under 20%; add a frozen copy and reset when it wins 70% of matches or after a step timeout. Alternate its reset target ROA-Star style (about 30% from the current main agent, 50% from a base or archetype-conditioned start, 20% unconditioned). Optionally one league exploiter over all frozen players with a 25% reset chance.
5. Snapshots: add the main agent to the league on a fixed cadence (AlphaStar every 2 × 10^9 steps, TStarBot-X every ~12 h; for 48 h, a few hours per snapshot gives roughly 10 to 20 entries).
6. Meta-solver: none needed for the bot pool beyond f_hard or a Hedge/regret-matching weight on per-opponent losses (the Anytime/SP-PSRO no-regret idea). Use a Nash or α-Rank solve over learned snapshots only if picking a final mixture.
7. Gate and selection: pick the snapshot with the best minimum per-opponent Wilson lower bound; plan about 130 to 200 evaluation matches per opponent (about 3,000 to 4,700 matches total for 23 opponents at a 90% to 88% observed rate) before claiming the 80% gate under a family-wise 95% bound.

### Gaps
- No published result shows that a league or PSRO method reaches a per-opponent win-rate floor against amateur HUNL bots in short tournament matches; the recipe is an extrapolation from StarCraft leagues and Leduc results.
- No small-compute HUNL league ablation (agent counts, mixture ratios, reset thresholds) was found; AlphaHoldem's own ablations were not retrievable.
- Throughput on the 20-core box (matches per hour in pure Python) was not measured and decides how many league members fit in 48 hours.
